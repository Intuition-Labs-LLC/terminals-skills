# Things /mint can make

`/mint` writes a finished thing you can keep — a plan, a doc, a decision, a checklist. Two more shapes are worth naming on their own, because they carry their honesty inside them.

## A hypothesis — a hunch you can hand someone

Sometimes the bench doesn't hold yet. The right thing to make then is not a fake answer — it's an honest hunch, made solid enough to test.

A hypothesis is a small made thing that says:

- **the claim** — one plain line you think might be true, said so it can be proven wrong.
- **the phase** — `hunch`, always. A hypothesis never wears a done badge. That is the whole point of it.
- **the hole it fills** — which gap in the work this would close.
- **the test** — the one thing you could look at that would move it from hunch toward real, or kill it. No test, no hypothesis. That is the contract.
- **the witness** — the `r` and `phi` of the converge it came out of, and the plain note: this is a hunch, here is how to check it.

Finding the holes is the other side of converge. Converge tells you what holds; read the same receipt backwards for what doesn't — the loose trios, the partials, the pairs that wouldn't lock. Those are the holes. Mint a hypothesis for each. Point it at your own body of work and it hands you back the next questions worth asking — honest by build, because it can't fake the answer it doesn't have yet.

## A finding — an edge you hit

When you use the bench and it gives a wrong or shaky answer, that's worth keeping. A finding is the made record of it:

- **the condition** — the case where it went wrong, in one plain line.
- **the smallest way to see it again** — the ideas and the scores that misbehaved, trimmed to the least that still shows it.
- **the fix it points at** — what the general solution might be, if you can name it.
- **why it's worth keeping** — most of what you read is just stuff: noise, but still something, never empty space. Now and then a bit of it tells you how to act. A finding is you keeping that bit.

A finding is the thing the repo's issue template publishes in one click, so the next version of the bench handles your edge in its convergence checks. Your real use makes the tool truer for everyone — that's the loop.

## The emit-step (design note, not yet wired)

The cleaner home for findings is a step in the verbs themselves: when an interaction crosses from noise into meaning — when `r`/`phi` says "this one told me how to act" — the verb emits a structured finding, and that emission is what the one-click report carries. It's spec'd here, not yet wired into the verb files. Wiring it touches every verb, so it waits for a deliberate pass, not a drive-by edit.
