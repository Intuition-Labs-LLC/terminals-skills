# The workbench

Picture a small bench with a few good tools on it.

You sit down with a mess. A pile of notes, a hard call, three options that fight. You pick up a tool. You tinker. You make a small thing you can hold and turn over in your hands. That small thing is a **trinket**. You can pick up the next tool and work it some more, or set it down because it already holds.

That is the whole shop. The tools are the words you type. The thing you make is a trinket. And every trinket comes with a tag that says whether it holds. A tool here never stamps a trinket "done" to be nice. If it only half-holds, the tag says **partial**, out loud. That honesty is the point of the bench.

## What sits where

The bench is plain words laid over the real names. Here is what is what.

| on the bench | the real name | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| a **tool** | a word (`/explore`, `/converge`, …) | the thing you pick up to do one job |
| a **trinket** | a **sematon** | the small made thing you hold when the tool is done |
| **"does it hold?"** | **R**, the agree-meter | 0 = it falls apart, 1 = every part agrees and it holds |
| **"does it work for a real person?"** | the gate | the trinket gets tried against the real world, not just itself |
| **the join between two tools** | a **gluon** | the bit of glue that holds one tool's trinket to the next tool's input |
| **whose bench it is** | the lens | your stuff, your context, set up the way *you* work |

Two of these are easy to mix up, so hold them apart.

**R is the inside check.** Does the trinket hold *together*? Do its own parts agree? When R hits 1, every part has locked in. Nothing rattles. That is done = true.

**The gate is the outside check.** A trinket can hold together perfectly and still be the wrong thing to ship. R = 1 says it is sound. The gate asks the harder thing: does it actually work out there, for someone, in the world? A trinket that holds is ready to take to the gate. Passing the gate is yours to judge.

The bench is yours. Point a tool at your own folder, your own notes, your half-made call, and it works your stuff. That is the lens. The more of your real work the bench sees, the better the trinket fits the way you actually think.

## The seven tools, at the bench

Each word is a tool on your workbench. Here is what each one does, in one line.

| tool | at the bench |
|---|---|
| `/frame` | Set up your bench from your own stuff: your folder, your notes, your half-made call. |
| `/explore` | Lay all the parts out, spread wide, so you can see every one before you pick. |
| `/converge` | Fit the parts into one trinket that holds, and hand you the receipt that says it holds. |
| `/optimize` | File off the rough edges: the same trinket, lighter and cleaner, still holding. |
| `/recommend` | You pick the tool, it does the work and hands you the finished trinket, with the receipt. |
| `/act` | Put the trinket to work through the tools you already have, asking you first. |
| `/hold` | Pick the trinket back up later and check it still holds when the world has moved. |

When a trinket only half-holds, the tool says so. It never fakes done = true.

## Putting tools together

You rarely need just one tool. The good part is how they combine, and there are two ways.

**One tool after another, in sequence.** You work a trinket with one tool, then pick up the next and work it some more. Open the mess up with one tool, bring it together with the next, trim it with a third. Each tool hands its trinket to the one after it. The join between them, the bit of glue that holds one tool's trinket to the next tool's input, is a **gluon**. A clean run is a row of trinkets, each held to the next by a gluon, ending in one thing that holds.

**Two tools at once, side by side.** Sometimes two jobs do not depend on each other, so you run them at the same time, side by side, and bring the results together after. Neither waits on the other. You set both trinkets on the bench and join them when both are done.

That is the grammar. In sequence, or side by side. Everything you build on this bench is some mix of those two: tools in a row, tools side by side, trinkets held together by gluons, every one of them carrying a tag that says, plainly, whether it holds.
